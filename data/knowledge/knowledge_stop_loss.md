# What Is a Stop-Loss Order?

As the name suggests, a stop-loss order is an order you place to stop your loss. Defining it by its own plain meaning is genuinely the best way to think about it — it's an order whose entire job is to limit how much money you can lose on a trade.

When you enter a trade, the money you're putting at risk is exactly that — a risk. If the trade starts moving against you, you want to have already decided, before it happens, how much you're willing to lose. If you're trading with $20, you might decide that if the trade's value falls to $15, you want out — no further losses beyond that point.

A stop-loss order is what makes that decision automatic. Instead of having to watch the price constantly and sell manually the moment it hits your limit, you place the stop-loss order in advance, at $15 in this example, and it executes on its own the instant the price reaches that level — even if you're not watching the market at that exact moment.

This connects directly to the risk/reward ratio: the distance between your entry price and your stop-loss price *is* your risk — it's the exact number used on the risk side of the ratio calculation. Choosing a stop-loss price isn't just about limiting losses; it's also one of the three numbers that determines what risk/reward ratio a trade actually has.
