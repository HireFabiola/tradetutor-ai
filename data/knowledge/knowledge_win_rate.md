# What Is Win Rate?

Win rate is how often a trade like the one you're considering actually works out — meaning it hits its target price instead of its stop-loss price. If a trade has a 60% win rate, that means trades with a similar setup have historically succeeded about 6 times out of every 10.

Win rate is a separate idea from the risk/reward ratio, and it's easy to mix the two up. The risk/reward ratio only tells you what happens *if* a trade succeeds — how much you'd gain compared to what you risked. It says nothing about *how likely* that success actually is. Win rate is what fills in that missing piece.

This is exactly why a great-looking ratio can still be part of a bad trade. A risk/reward ratio of 5-to-1 sounds excellent on paper, but if trades like it only succeed 10% of the time, most attempts would end in a loss. A more modest ratio of 2-to-1 paired with a much higher win rate could be the safer, more reliable choice overall.

Because of this, an experienced trader weighs the ratio and the likelihood of success together, rather than judging a trade by its ratio alone. A high ratio is only genuinely attractive when the odds of it actually succeeding are reasonable too.
